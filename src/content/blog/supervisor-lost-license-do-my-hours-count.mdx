## Two different questions, not one

"My supervisor lost their license, do my hours still count" sounds like a single
question. It isn't. There are two very different situations hiding inside it,
and the answer depends entirely on which one you're facing.

The first: your supervisor's credential lapsed, expired, or was suspended
**during** the period they were supervising you. Several states address this
directly, and the answer is often no, those hours don't count.

The second: your supervisor's license is revoked or disciplined **after** your
hours were already logged, signed, and submitted, sometimes years later, for
something unrelated to your supervision at all. This one is much less settled,
and pretending otherwise would be dishonest. Most state rule texts simply
don't say.

Knowing which situation you're in changes what you should do next, so it's
worth separating them before you panic or assume the worst.

## When the credential lapses during your supervision

Some states write this rule in plain language. [North Carolina's](/states/nc-lcmhca)
LCMHCA supervision requirements state that a supervisor must hold the LCMHCS
credential specifically, and that credential is itself subject to periodic
renewal. The rule file we maintain for NC includes this exact warning:
supervisors must maintain their LCMHCS credential throughout the supervision
period, and a lapsed LCMHCS credential invalidates the supervision logged
under it. That's not a hedge or a guess. It's how the requirement is written.

What makes this dangerous is the timing. A supervisor's credential can lapse
quietly, sometimes over a renewal window they simply forgot to file, and
nothing about the supervision session itself looks any different. You show
up, you meet, you log the hour. The problem doesn't surface until someone
checks the credential dates against the session dates, which is often not
until the board reviews your file for licensure.

[Washington's](/states/wa-lmhca) LMHCA rule takes a different approach. It
requires a supervisor to be licensed without restrictions and in good
standing for the two years before they take on a supervisee (WAC
246-809-234(1)). That's a real, board-verifiable qualification bar, but it's
framed at the point supervision begins. The rule as written doesn't spell out
what happens if a supervisor's standing changes partway through an existing
supervision relationship, the way North Carolina's does for a lapsed LCMHCS
credential. That's a meaningful difference if you're trying to figure out
where you stand, and it's exactly the kind of gap you should confirm with
your own board rather than assume one way or the other.

## The murkier scenario: revoked after your hours were already signed

This is the part of the question that most guidance online skips past,
usually by implying an answer the rule text doesn't contain. So here's the
honest version: across the state rule sets we track, including North Carolina
and Washington, none of them give an explicit, board-stated answer for what
happens to hours you already logged and had signed off if your supervisor's
license is later revoked for conduct unrelated to your supervision.

That's a genuine gap, not an oversight on our part. Some boards may treat
already-completed, already-approved hours as settled once your file was
accepted. Others may reopen anything supervised by that person if the
revocation touches on their fitness to have been supervising at all. The rule
texts we've reviewed simply don't say, and inventing a clean answer here would
be worse than admitting the uncertainty.

If you're in this situation, or worried you might be, the right move is to
ask your board directly, in writing, and get the answer specific to your
license type and your state. Don't rely on a forum post, a general guide, or
what happened to someone else in a different state. Boards vary, and this is
one of the areas where they have discretion the written rule doesn't resolve
for you in advance.

## What you can do before it becomes your problem

Most of the risk here is a timing problem, not a rules problem. You can't
control whether your supervisor's credential lapses. You can control how fast
you find out.

**Check your supervisor's license status more than once.** Verifying it when
supervision starts confirms day one. It tells you nothing about month
fourteen. Most state boards run a public license lookup that takes under a
minute to check. Put a recurring reminder on your calendar, quarterly is
reasonable, and check the credential type as well as the status. A lapsed
renewal often shows up as "expired" before it shows up as anything more
serious.

**Ask, don't assume.** If your supervisor mentions a break in practice, a
move to a new setting, or anything that sounds like it could touch their
credential, ask directly whether their supervisory designation is still
current. It's a normal question, not an accusatory one.

**Keep your own record of when you checked.** A screenshot or saved
confirmation with a date on it is worth more than your memory of "I think I
checked a few months ago" if a board ever asks you to reconstruct your
timeline.

**Get the specifics if something does change.** If you learn your
supervisor's status changed, get the exact date, not a vague description.
"Sometime last spring" doesn't help you or the board figure out which
sessions are affected. An exact date does.
