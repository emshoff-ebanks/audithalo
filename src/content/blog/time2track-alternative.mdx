## What Time2Track actually is

Before comparing anything, it's worth being precise about what Time2Track is,
since a lot of people searching "Time2Track alternative" got pointed to it by
a school or a supervisor and never looked closely at what it was built for.

Time2Track markets itself as a web-based experience management system, and it
covers a wide range of training fields at once: psychology, marriage and
family therapy, counseling, teacher education, school psychology, public
health, and legal internships all show up as named use cases on its site. It
does have a page aimed at LPC and counseling hours specifically, describing
itself there as a way for "counseling students, interns, and professionals to
track LPC hours." So it isn't a tool built for a different profession
entirely, the way, say, a pure BCBA fieldwork tracker would be. It's a
cross-discipline hours logger that counseling happens to be one of several
categories under.

That matters for what it does and doesn't do. A tool built to serve law
interns, school psychologists, and MFT trainees under one product can't bake
in any single state's mental-health-counseling licensure rule as a first
principle, because most of its users aren't operating under that rule at all.
Reviewing its public feature and pricing pages, we found no mention of
state-specific licensure citations, a rules engine, sealed evidence packages,
tamper-evident signatures, or audit-trail security. What it does describe is
hour and activity logging, custom report generation, progress charts, and
signature lines for a supervisor to sign a printed report. Individual pricing
runs $9.99 a month billed monthly, or $5.99 a month billed annually, with a
30-day free trial.

None of that makes it a bad hour log. It's a reasonable, affordable way to
keep a running count of training activities across a lot of different fields.
But an hour log and a compliance system solve different problems, and the gap
between them is exactly what tends to surface at renewal or audit time.

## What a general hours tracker checks, and what it doesn't

A cross-discipline logging tool is built to answer one question well: how
many hours of what type have been entered. A second, harder question sits
underneath it: does this specific hour, under this specific state's
mental-health-counseling rule, actually count. A general tool built for many
fields at once has no single rule to check it against.

Those are different jobs. A supervisee can log fifty hours under a supervisor
whose credential lapsed partway through, and a generic tracker has no reason
to flag it, because it isn't evaluating supervisor qualification against a
licensure board's requirement. It can let group supervision hours pile up
past whatever share a specific state allows for individual supervision,
because it isn't tracking that ratio against a rule either. The tool did its
job: it recorded what was entered. Nobody checked whether what was entered
was actually valid until a board reviewer did, months or years later.

## Sealed, tamper-evident evidence packages

Printing a report with a signature line solves the signing problem but not
the proof problem. A printed PDF with a supervisor's signature can still be
edited by anyone with access to the file it came from, and a board reviewing
it years later has no way to know whether it was. AuditHalo seals every fully
signed session into an [evidence package](/evidence-packages): supervisor
credential snapshot, citation to the rule in force at the time of signing,
both signatures with intent confirmation, all hashed with SHA-256. Change one
field afterward and the hash stops matching. Anyone with the verify link, no
account required, can confirm a package still matches what was sealed. That's
a different kind of guarantee than a document someone printed and signed.

## At-risk flags before a gap becomes permanent

Some rule problems are fixable if you catch them early and unrecoverable once
a deadline passes. A lapsed supervisor credential can sometimes be resolved
by re-supervising the affected window. A hard forfeiture rule, once
triggered, usually can't be undone. AuditHalo's dashboard tracks
setting-approval status and the running individual-supervision share on an
ongoing basis, and surfaces an at-risk flag up to 60 days before a deadline
would turn a fixable gap into a permanent one. Supervisors see it on the
roster view. Nobody has to wait for an annual review, or a report generated
after the fact, to find out something was off.

## FAQ

**Is Time2Track built specifically for mental-health-counseling licensure?**
No. It's a hours-tracking tool used across several training fields,
including psychology, MFT, counseling, school psychology, and legal
internships. Its counseling-focused page describes tracking LPC hours, but
its features are general-purpose activity logging rather than a rule set
built around any one state's licensure requirements.

**Does Time2Track check state-specific supervision rules?**
Based on its public feature and pricing pages, no. It describes hour
logging, custom reports, and progress graphs, not rule citations, supervisor
credential checks, or evidence-sealing.

**Do I have to already be behind on hours to switch tools?**
No. Switching is easiest before a deadline is close, since AuditHalo's rule
evaluation and evidence sealing apply going forward from the point you start
logging sessions in it.
