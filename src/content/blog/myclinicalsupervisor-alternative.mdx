If you typed "MyClinicalSupervisor alternative" into a search bar, you're
probably not shopping for a brand name. You're shopping for a way to stop
reconstructing supervision records under deadline pressure, or a way to
catch a problem with someone's hours before a state board finds it first.
That's the right instinct, and it's worth answering directly: the name on

## Four questions to ask before you commit to a tool

Most supervision-tracking software looks similar on a pricing page: log
sessions, get signatures, export a report. The differences that actually
matter show up later, usually at renewal or audit time. Ask these before
you commit to one:

1. Does the tool know your state's specific rule, or does it just add up
   hours you enter?
2. What happens to a session record after both people sign it? Can it be
   edited later, by anyone?
3. Does it flag a developing problem while there's still time to fix it, or
   only report a total at the end?
4. Does it check who was actually allowed to supervise at the time, or just
   record that supervision happened?

## Generic hour-logging tools don't evaluate what they log

A lot of supervision trackers are, underneath the branding, a timesheet
with a clinical label on it: date, duration, session type, a signature
field. That's a reasonable floor, and it beats a spreadsheet. But logging
that a session happened is a different task from confirming that the
session actually qualifies under your specific state's rule. A tool that
only does the first one will happily let you log fifty hours under a
supervisor whose credential lapsed in month two, or count group sessions
past the share your state allows for individual supervision. The gap
doesn't show up until someone checks the log against the rule, which is
usually the board, at the worst possible time.

## Sealed, tamper-evident evidence packages

Logging and signing a session is only half the problem. The other half is
proving it later, sometimes years later, to a board that has never seen
your file before. AuditHalo seals every fully signed session into an
[evidence package](/evidence-packages): supervisor credential snapshot,
citation to the rule current at the time of signing, both signatures with
intent confirmation, all SHA-256 hashed. Change a single field afterward
and the hash no longer matches, which means the board doesn't have to take
your word for the record's integrity. Anyone with the verify-URL, no
account needed, can confirm the package still matches its sealed hash.
That's a meaningfully different guarantee than a PDF export that anyone
with edit access could quietly alter.

## At-risk flags, before a gap becomes a forfeiture

Some rule violations are fixable if caught early and unfixable once a
deadline passes. A lapsed supervisor credential can sometimes be resolved
by re-supervising the affected window. A hard forfeiture rule, once
triggered, usually can't be undone at all. The dashboard tracks
setting-approval status and the running individual-supervision share
continuously, and surfaces an at-risk flag up to 60 days before a deadline
would turn a fixable gap into a permanent one. Supervisors see this on the
roster view; nobody has to wait for an annual review or a board letter to
find out something is off.

## Supervisor credential validation at signing

A session supervised by someone whose credential has lapsed is a session
at risk, regardless of how good the supervision itself was. AuditHalo
captures and validates a supervisor credential snapshot at the moment of
signing, not at renewal. If a credential has expired, was suspended, or
never qualified as a supervisory designation to begin with, that surfaces
as a flag immediately, tied to the specific session it affects, instead of
surfacing as a board finding on someone else's timeline.

## Switching doesn't require you to already be behind

You don't need a board audit already in progress to make a change. Most
switches happen for a quieter reason: a supervisor notices they can't
answer a specific question quickly, like which supervisee is closest to an
individual-supervision floor, or which sessions were signed by a
supervisor whose credential has since changed. Moving a roster over means
entering supervisees, their state, and their start dates. Past sessions
logged elsewhere can be noted for context, but AuditHalo's rule evaluation
and evidence sealing apply going forward, on sessions logged and signed
inside the platform. If you're mid-obligation, the earlier you move the
more of the remaining timeline is actually covered.
