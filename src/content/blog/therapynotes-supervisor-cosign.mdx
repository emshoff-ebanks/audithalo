## TherapyNotes is a real EHR with a real supervision feature

If your supervisee's practice runs on TherapyNotes, you already know it's not a
toy. It's one of the more established EHRs for behavioral health practices,
and it has an actual supervision workflow built into user roles, not a
bolted-on afterthought. This post isn't arguing you should replace it. It's
about a narrower question: what does a TherapyNotes supervisor cosign
confirm, and what does your state licensing board need that a note
cosign was never built to produce.

## What TherapyNotes' supervision feature does

Worth being precise here rather than guessing at a competitor's feature set.
Based on TherapyNotes' own support documentation and release notes, here's
what the supervision workflow covers:

- **Supervisor assignment.** Any user can be assigned as a Supervisor over
  specific Clinicians or Interns, attached at the case level so the
  supervisor's access lines up with who they oversee.
- **Three supervision levels.** A practice sets one of three modes per
  supervisee: "view only," where the supervisor can read notes but isn't
  asked to sign anything; "must review and approve," which puts a note on the
  supervisor's To-Do list before it's entered into the client record; and
  "must review and co-sign," which requires the supervisor's signature
  before the note is finalized.
- **Cosigning tied to billing.** When a note requires a cosign, that
  signature is also what allows the session to be billed under the
  supervisor's credentials, the incident-to arrangement many practices rely
  on for pre-licensed clinicians. A 2024 release (version 4.33) extended this
  further, letting claims list both the clinician's and the supervisor's
  provider details, or bill entirely under the supervisor, depending on
  payer rules.
- **Audit trail on the note itself.** Each cosigned note carries a record of
  who wrote it, who approved or signed it, and when, inside TherapyNotes'
  standard documentation history.

That's a real, useful workflow, and it's built around a specific
question: was this note reviewed, is it accurate, and can it be billed
correctly. Nothing in TherapyNotes' public documentation describes a
separate feature for tallying supervision hours by category or checking
those totals against a state's licensing rule. If that exists as an internal
report we haven't confirmed, it isn't described anywhere in TherapyNotes'
support site or release notes, and it isn't the workflow the cosign feature
is designed around.

## What a note cosign proves, and what it doesn't

A cosigned note is real evidence of something specific: a licensed
supervisor read a piece of clinical documentation, evaluated it, and put
their name on it. For clinical quality and billing compliance, that matters
a lot. Insurance payers who allow incident-to billing generally require
exactly this kind of sign-off before they'll pay a claim tied to a
supervisor's credentials.

But a licensing board isn't asking the question TherapyNotes' cosign
workflow answers. It's asking a different set of questions: how many hours
of individual supervision did this supervisee accrue against the total, does
the group-to-individual ratio fall inside what the state allows, was the
supervising clinician's license active and correctly designated on every
date supervision took place, and can you produce a record of all of it that
hasn't been altered after the fact. A stack of cosigned notes doesn't answer
any of those on its own. It shows review happened at the note level. It
doesn't show that the hours behind those notes clear a specific state
minimum, because that was never the job it was built for.

This is the same gap that shows up with [SimplePractice's cosign
feature](/blog/simplepractice-supervision-tracking): the workflow is
similar in shape, note gets written, note gets routed to a supervisor, note
gets signed, but neither product is built to also run every logged hour
through a state's administrative code and flag when the ratio or the total
falls short. That's a different engine, closer to a rules system than a
documentation system, and general EHRs aren't built to maintain fifty
different state rule sets inside a product serving every kind of practice.

## Why the two jobs don't merge cleanly

Think about what each system would need to track separately. A cosign
system needs to know: was this note written, is a supervisor attached, did
they sign it. A licensing-hour system needs to know: what type of hour was
this (individual, group, or indirect), does the ratio across all hours this
period meet the state floor, was the supervisor's credential valid and
correctly classified on this exact date, and is there a tamper-evident
record of both signatures once the entry is locked. Those aren't variations
on the same problem. Building the second on top of the first would mean an
EHR taking on the job of tracking every state board's citation and effective
date, which isn't what a general practice-management platform is for.
