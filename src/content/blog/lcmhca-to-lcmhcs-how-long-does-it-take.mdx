If you're partway through your LCMHCA hours and already picturing yourself
as a supervisor one day, the honest answer to "how long will that take" is
longer than most people expect, and it isn't a single number. It's a chain
of separate stages, each with its own clock, and none of them run at the
same time as the others. This post walks through the math. If you want the
background on what LCMHCS is and why North Carolina treats it as
a distinct credential from LCMHC, read [our companion piece on the LCMHCS
credential](/blog/lcmhcs-north-carolina-supervisor-credential-explained)
first. This one is about the timeline, not the definition.

## The four stages, laid end to end

**Stage 1: LCMHCA, 2 to 5 years.** Under [21 NCAC 53](https://www.ncblcmhc.org/licensure/),
an LCMHCA completes 3,000 hours of supervised practice, including at least
100 supervision hours, over a window the board sets at a minimum of 24
months and a maximum of 60 months. Two years is the fastest the rule
allows. Five years is the outer edge before hours logged past that window
may not count toward a single licensure application. Most candidates land
somewhere in between, depending on whether they're working full time
toward their hours or juggling a part-time caseload alongside other work.

**Stage 2: applying for and receiving full LCMHC licensure.** Once your
3,000 hours and 100 supervision hours are complete and verified, you apply
for LCMHC. This step has processing time attached to it, on the order of
weeks rather than years, but it's still a real gap between finishing your
hours and holding the license that starts the next clock.

**Stage 3: at least 2 years post-LCMHC before LCMHCS eligibility.** This is
where the timeline math gets less intuitive. The clock for LCMHCS
eligibility doesn't start when you begin your LCMHCA hours, and it doesn't
start when you submit your LCMHC application. It starts the day you're
issued full LCMHC licensure. From that date, the rule requires at least
two more years of post-LCMHC experience before you're eligible to apply
for the supervisor credential.

**Stage 4: the supervisor training course.** On top of the experience
requirement, LCMHCS also requires completion of a board-approved
supervisor training course. The rule text we track for North Carolina
doesn't publish an exact hour count for this course, so if you're planning
around it, confirm the current requirement directly with the [NC Board of
Licensed Clinical Mental Health Counselors](https://www.ncblcmhc.org/licensure/)
rather than assuming a number from a blog post, including this one. Some
sources online reference figures in the range of three graduate semester
credits or roughly 45 continuing-education hours, but we can't verify that
against the board's current rule text, so treat it as a starting point for
your own research, not a confirmed fact.

## Adding it up honestly

Here's the derived range, and it is a derived range, not a single figure
the board publishes:

- LCMHCA period: 2 to 5 years
- Post-LCMHC wait before LCMHCS eligibility: 2+ years
- Supervisor training course: unspecified duration in the rule text, likely weeks to a few months once you're eligible to take it

Add the first two together and you get a floor of roughly 4 years and a
realistic upper range of 7 or more years, before accounting for how long
the training course itself takes once you're eligible to enroll. That
"4 to 7+ years" range is the honest answer to "how long does LCMHCA to
LCMHCS take." Anyone who gives you a single number, like "5 years flat,"
is either rounding aggressively or guessing.

## A worked example, for illustration only

Numbers land better with a timeline attached, so here's one hypothetical
path. This is illustrative, not a figure the board publishes, and your own
path will vary based on how your LCMHCA hours and post-LCMHC experience
play out.

- **Year 0:** You start as an LCMHCA, contract filed and approved, hours
  begin counting.
- **Year 3:** You hit 3,000 practice hours and 100 supervision hours, near
  the faster end of the 2-to-5-year window, and apply for full LCMHC
  licensure.
- **Year 3, a few weeks later:** LCMHC is issued. The post-LCMHC clock for
  LCMHCS eligibility starts on this date, not before.
- **Year 5:** You've completed the required 2+ years of post-LCMHC
  experience and are now eligible to apply for LCMHCS, pending completion
  of the supervisor training course.
- **Year 5 to 5.5, roughly:** You complete the training course and submit
  your LCMHCS application.

In this fast-case scenario, the LCMHCA to LCMHCS path runs about 5 to 5.5
years. Someone who takes the full 5 years as an LCMHCA before reaching
LCMHC, or who delays starting the post-LCMHC training course, will land
closer to 7 or 8 years instead. The stages are fixed. Where you land inside
the range is mostly a function of how quickly each individual stage moves
for you.

## Why none of these stages compress

It's worth being direct about this, because it's the part people hope
isn't true: you cannot pursue LCMHCS-track experience while you're still
an LCMHCA. The two-year post-LCMHC clock in Stage 3 does not start early
just because you're a fast, capable LCMHCA who's already thinking about
supervising. It starts on the date your LCMHC is issued, full stop.

That means a slow Stage 1 doesn't just cost you time in Stage 1. It pushes
every later stage back by the same amount, because each stage's start date
depends on the previous one finishing. If your LCMHCA period runs 4 years
instead of 2, your entire path to LCMHCS moves 2 years later too. There's
no way to bank time from an earlier stage against a later one.

## What varies within that range

The parts of this timeline you can influence are mostly about pace and
documentation, not about skipping stages:

- **How consistently you log supervision hours.** North Carolina requires
  at least 1 hour of individual supervision every 40 practice hours, and
  gaps in that cadence can stall your progress even if your total hour
  count looks fine on paper. See [the full LCMHCA supervision
  requirements](/lcmhca-supervision-requirements) for the exact ratios.
- **Whether your supervision contract was filed and approved before you
  started logging hours.** Hours logged before board approval don't count,
  which can silently add months to your LCMHCA period if the contract
  filing lagged behind your start date.
- **Your supervisor's credential status.** If your supervisor's LCMHCS
  lapses partway through your supervision, hours logged during that gap
  are at risk, which can force you to make up time you thought you'd
  already banked.
- **How promptly you apply for LCMHC** once your hours are complete, since
  the post-LCMHC clock for LCMHCS doesn't start until the license is
  issued.

None of these change the 2-year minimum or the 5-year ceiling on the
LCMHCA stage, but they do determine whether you land closer to the floor
of the range or the ceiling.
