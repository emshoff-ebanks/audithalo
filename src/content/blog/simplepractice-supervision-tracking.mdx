## SimplePractice is not the problem

If your practice runs on SimplePractice, you already know why. It handles
scheduling, notes, billing, and client records well enough that most
clinicians never think about the software at all, which is the point of a
good EHR. This post isn't a case for switching away from it. It's about a
narrower question: what happens to your pre-licensed clinicians' supervision
hours once they're logged, and whether SimplePractice was ever built to
answer the specific compliance question your state licensing board asks.

## What SimplePractice actually does for supervision

Worth being precise here, since a lot of comparison content guesses at
competitor features instead of checking them. SimplePractice's supervision
tools, confirmed on its support site, cover:

- **Supervisor and supervisee designation.** You can mark a team member as a
  supervisor and assign supervisees to them. A supervisor can have an
  unlimited number of supervisees; a supervisee is limited to one supervisor
  at a time.
- **Note review and co-signing.** A supervisee writes and signs a
  progress note, sends it to their supervisor, and the supervisor can
  request edits or add their own signature once it's ready. There's a
  Supervision tab for finding notes still waiting on a co-sign.
- **Supervisor info on documents.** Practices can set supervisor
  information to auto-populate on notes and billing documents for
  pre-licensed clinicians, and bill insurance under the supervisor's
  credentials where that's permitted.
- **Care Aide's Note Checker**, available to pre-licensed supervisees,
  reviews a progress note's content against general documentation standards
  and gives feedback before it goes to the supervisor.

That's a real, useful workflow. It answers "was this note reviewed and
signed by the right person" for an individual session. It's built around
documentation quality and billing correctness, which is exactly what an EHR
should be built around.

## What it isn't built to answer

Search SimplePractice's own user community and you'll find clinicians asking
a different question: whether there's a way to track accumulating direct
client hours, individual and group supervision hours, and indirect hours
separately, then sign off on the totals and export them for a licensing
application. That thread reads like a feature request, not a documented
capability, which tells you something. Tracking a note's review status and
tracking a supervisee's progress against a state's specific hour
requirements are different jobs, and general practice-management platforms
are mostly built for the first one.

A licensing board doesn't ask "was every note co-signed." It asks things
like: how many of these hours were individual versus group, does that ratio
meet the state minimum, was the supervisor's credential valid on every date
supervision occurred, and can you produce a record that hasn't been altered
since it was signed. Many general practice-management platforms include
note co-signing but aren't built to also evaluate every logged hour against
your state's specific supervision ratio, credential requirements, and
deadline structure. That's a rules problem, not a documentation problem, and
it needs a different kind of engine.

## Why the two problems don't merge cleanly

A co-signed note proves supervision happened and that a licensed person
reviewed the work. It doesn't tell you whether that hour counted toward an
individual-supervision floor your state sets, or whether the supervising
clinician's credential was still active and correctly designated on that
date. Those checks require a system that knows the actual administrative
code for the state the supervisee is licensed in, not just a signature
field. Building that into a general EHR would mean maintaining fifty
different rule sets inside a product meant to serve every kind of practice,
which isn't its job.

## Using both together

Nothing here requires picking one tool over the other. The practical setup
most supervisors land on: keep clinical notes, scheduling, and billing in
SimplePractice, since it's already integrated into daily clinical work and
your clients' records live there. Log supervision sessions, hour types, and
supervisor sign-offs in AuditHalo, where each entry gets evaluated against
the actual state rule and sealed once both parties confirm it. The two
systems don't need to talk to each other. They cover different halves of the
same supervisee's record: one proves the clinical work happened and was
reviewed, the other proves the supervision hours meet the specific
requirement your board will check.

Think of it by what each system is asked at renewal time. SimplePractice can
show that a note existed, was reviewed, and carries a supervisor's
signature. It can't tell a board whether that supervisee's individual hours
cleared the floor their state sets, whether a given week's group session
exceeded the share allowed to count, or whether the supervising clinician's
license was active on the date they signed. Those are the questions
AuditHalo's evaluation is built to answer, session by session, as hours
accumulate rather than after the fact.
